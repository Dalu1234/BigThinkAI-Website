Does it obey the world?

Video world models can render a plausible scene. These benchmarks check whether the scene behaves: gears that mesh at the right ratio, a swing that loses energy the way physics says it should, a mirror that remembers what it reflected, a cut that leaves the right pieces.

01 / Mechanics

Gear interaction

01

Each gear and its white tooth mark are tracked frame by frame. The evaluator checks rotation direction, the tooth-count ratio between meshed gears, where the marked tooth stops, whether every tooth survives, and image clarity. One required check failing is a fail, even when the weighted score is high.

    04 / Object permanence

    Channel cuts

    04

    A knife makes a specified cut and then withdraws. Using the white cube as a ruler, the evaluator measures every piece and gap, checks that pieces plus kerf add back up to the uncut object, and follows the red and blue identity caps. Five reference cuts pass; seven broken rod cuts each fail for their own reason.

    05 / Method

    How a clip is judged

    05

    Every family follows the same pattern. Nothing on this page comes from a world model: each example is a rendered control or a deliberately broken variant, built to show that the evaluator separates right from wrong.

    01

    Prompt

    A fixed camera, a starting image, and a precise instruction with measurable quantities.

    02

    Track

    Markers, gears, spheres or pieces are located in each decoded frame. No hidden simulator state.

    03

    Measure

    Angles, ratios, peak heights, positions and timing are compared against an independent reference.

    04

    Decide

    Weighted scores plus hard gates: a physically impossible result fails or is capped, whatever else looks right.
